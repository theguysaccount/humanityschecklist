At the top of Humanity's Checklist sits a single item: the story we tell ourselves. Not climate, not poverty, not war. A story. That can sound soft until you notice how much of what people do depends on what they expect.

## People act out the story they believe

In 1948 the sociologist Robert K. Merton gave a name to something everyone has seen: the **self-fulfilling prophecy**. His example was a sound bank that collapses because a rumor says it will. Depositors believe the rumor, rush to withdraw their money and make it true.

Whole societies work the same way. If people believe the future will be worse, they invest less in it. They have fewer reasons to cooperate, to build or to take risks for others. The belief produces the decline it predicted.

## The story we are telling now

The dominant story today is one of decline. Pew Research Center surveys have found majorities in many wealthy countries expecting today's children to grow up financially worse off than their parents. Our films and series are full of collapsed futures. Our feeds reward alarm. Many young people say they feel the world is ending.

There are real reasons for concern. But a story of inevitable collapse is not a neutral description. It is a force that makes collapse more likely by telling people that effort is pointless.

## Utopia, dystopia, protopia

Stories about the future usually come in two kinds. **Utopias** promise a perfect world, which is impossible and often turns sinister when people try to impose it. **Dystopias** predict collapse, which breeds fear and passivity.

In 2011 the writer Kevin Kelly proposed a third word: **protopia**. A protopia is a world that is a little better today than it was yesterday. Not perfect, and never finished, but improving through steady, compounding progress. Protopia is less dramatic than either alternative, which is exactly why it works. It gives people something they can act on this year.

## The new story

Humanity's Checklist tells a specific version of that story. Humanity is one superorganism learning to heal itself. Every problem on Earth is solvable. Our problems are symptoms of a few sources, and those sources can be flipped. Protopia is something we build together.

This story is not wishful thinking. It is a working hypothesis that changes behavior. People who believe a problem can be solved go looking for collaborators, funders and ideas. That is why the story sits above coordination on the triangle: it is what makes people bother to coordinate at all.

## Telling it at scale

The beachhead for the story is the story itself, told at scale in the places people already listen. The checklist is meant to be its first page: a short, shareable map that turns overwhelm into six things that can actually be done.

Anyone can work on this item today. Every time someone shares a story of a problem being solved, a person finding their collaborators or a community choosing to build, the dominant story shifts a little. That is protopia in practice: a little better than yesterday.
