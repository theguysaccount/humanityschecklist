People love to ask what it would cost to fix the world. Ending hunger, stopping climate change, curing a disease: each comes with a price tag in the hundreds of billions or trillions, and the size of the number becomes the reason it never happens.

Here is a different answer. The cost to save humanity is zero. Not because the work is free, but because everything it needs is already being spent.

## The budget already exists

Look at what humanity spends every year without blinking.

- **More than $300 billion** on designer goods.
- **More than $500 billion** given to charity in the United States alone.
- **Around $1 trillion** on advertising worldwide, mostly to capture attention.
- **More than two hours a day**, on average, from around five billion people on social media.
- **The world economy** produces well over $100 trillion of goods and services a year.

The money, time, attention and talent needed to solve most world problems are already flowing. They are just flowing somewhere else. As the [whitepaper](/whitepaper/) puts it, the world does not have a resource problem. It has a coordination, vision and status problem.

## Redirect, don't raise

If the resources exist, saving humanity is not a fundraising problem. It is a routing problem. The question changes from "how much do we need?" to "what would make the money, time and attention already in motion flow toward repair?"

That kind of change costs nothing extra, because it does not ask anyone to spend more. It changes where the spending goes.

There are already small examples:

- **Ecosia**, a search engine founded in 2009, uses the advertising revenue from ordinary searches to fund tree planting. Users search as they always would; the money goes somewhere new.
- **Wikipedia** turned spare hours that people would have spent anyway into one of the largest reference works in history.
- **Open source** software turned developers' existing skills into shared infrastructure that most of the internet now runs on.

None of these asked people to sacrifice. They built a platform that pointed existing behavior at a better target.

## The platforms that change everything else

So the real question is: which platforms change everything else? Humanity's Checklist answers with six, one for each source that sits upstream of every world problem.

| Existing flow | Where it goes now | Platform that redirects it |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Feeds tuned for outrage | [Bloomscroll](/blog/doomscrolling-to-bloomscrolling/), a feed ranked for the value it adds |
| Status spending | Designer goods that signal wealth | [Charity designer goods](/blog/giving-as-the-new-status-symbol/) that signal giving |
| Networking time | Résumés and job titles | [The Social Router](/blog/every-problem-is-a-coordination-problem/), routing people by vision |
| Imagination | Dystopias | [The Vision Market](/blog/the-vision-market/), where people back better futures |
| Creative talent | Gatekept platforms | [The Co-Creator Portal](/blog/the-co-creator-portal/) |
| The story | Decline and doom | [A new story of protopia](/blog/protopia-the-story-we-tell-ourselves/) |

Each of these platforms can pay for itself. Charity designer goods are sold, so buyers fund them. A better feed still earns revenue. A router and a vision market create value for the people who use them. Once they run, they redirect flows worth billions without needing a permanent budget.

## So why $5 million?

Humanity's Checklist asks [what if $5 million was all it took](/blog/what-if-5-million-was-all-it-took/). That number is the seed, not the cost. It is what it takes to start six platforms that then redirect what already flows. Spread across the resources they could steer, the net cost to humanity rounds to zero.

## The question that matters

Asking what it costs to save the world keeps the conversation stuck on scarcity. Asking which platforms change everything else moves it to leverage. The first question has no good answer. The second has six, and you can start on any of them today.
