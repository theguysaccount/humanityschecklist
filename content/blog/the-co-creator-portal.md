The internet was supposed to let anyone build anything. In many ways it did. But over time, a few chokepoints came to decide what actually gets built and who gets seen: the big platforms, the app stores, the ranking algorithms and the investors who fund what runs on top of them.

Humanity's Checklist calls these the gatekeepers. They are the hands of the human superorganism, and right now most of those hands are tied.

## Who the gatekeepers are

The word is not just a metaphor. The European Union's Digital Markets Act, adopted in 2022, formally designates the largest platforms as "gatekeepers" and places special rules on them because of how much control they hold over access to users.

Gatekeeping shows up in several ways:

- **Platforms** set the rules for what can be posted, sold and promoted.
- **App stores** decide which apps reach phones and take a cut of sales, historically up to 30 percent.
- **Ranking algorithms** decide which creators are seen and which are invisible.
- **Investors** decide which ideas get the money to become real, and most of that money flows through a small number of networks and cities.

None of this is a conspiracy. It is a structure. But structures shape outcomes.

## Why it matters for every problem

Most good ideas never ship. Not because they are bad, but because the people who have them cannot get past the gates: they lack the network, the funding, the platform approval or the reach. Every solution that dies at the gate is a symptom that keeps going.

When creation runs as a hierarchy, the intelligence of the whole is limited to the intelligence of whoever controls the bottlenecks.

## Hierarchy vs. collective intelligence

There is a working alternative, and it has already produced some of the most important things on the internet.

- **Wikipedia**, launched in 2001, became one of the largest reference works in history through open collaboration.
- **Linux**, started in 1991, grew into software that runs much of the world's servers, built by contributors spread around the world.
- **Open source** software now sits inside nearly every modern application.

These projects show that when anyone can contribute, and good contributions are recognized, groups of people can build things no hierarchy would have planned. That is collective intelligence.

## The Co-Creator Portal

The Co-Creator Portal is the checklist's beachhead for gatekeepers: shared tools that let anyone imagine, build and launch digital experiences together.

- **Open to anyone**, with no gate between having an idea and starting to build it.
- **Built for teams**, so people routed together by the Social Router have a place to work.
- **Fed by visions**, so builders can pick up the futures previewed in the Vision Market.
- **Credit for every contributor**, so collaboration is rewarded rather than captured.

New tools make this more realistic than ever. AI assistants now help people with no formal training write code, design interfaces and produce media. The barrier to building has never been lower. The barrier to being seen and funded has not moved as much. The portal is aimed at that second barrier.

## This site is a small example

Humanity's Checklist practices what it proposes. The site is open source. Anyone, including an AI agent, can propose a change by following the instructions in the repository's AGENTS.md file and opening a pull request. No gatekeeper decides who may improve it.

Open the gates, and the hands of humanity can build what its imagination already sees.
