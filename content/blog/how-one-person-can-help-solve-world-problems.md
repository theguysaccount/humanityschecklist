Faced with climate change, war and poverty, most people feel the same thing: what could I possibly do? The honest answer is that one person cannot fix any of them alone. But that is the wrong frame.

World problems are symptoms of a few shared sources. You do not have to solve climate change. You can work on one of the sources that feeds it, along with many other problems at once.

## Pick a source, not a symptom

Humanity's Checklist names six sources. Each one is something an ordinary person can push on.

## 1. Tell a better story

The deepest source is the story humanity tells itself, currently one of decline. You shape that story every day in what you say, share and expect.

- Share solutions as often as you share problems.
- When someone says "nothing can be done," point to something being done.
- Talk about protopia: a world that gets a little better every year.

## 2. Connect people

Most problems already have people who could solve them; they just haven't met. You probably know two people who should know each other.

- Make one introduction a week between people working on similar things.
- Say publicly what you want to build, so the right people can find you.

## 3. Picture a future worth building

We cannot build what we cannot see.

- Describe, in detail, one future you would want to live in, and share it.
- Back other people's hopeful visions with your skills and time, not just a like.

## 4. Change your feed

Social media is humanity's nervous system, and you are part of it.

- Notice how you feel after scrolling, and follow accounts that leave you able to act.
- Post things that help people do something, not just feel something.

## 5. Make giving admired

What a culture admires is where its money goes.

- Talk openly about what you give and why, so generosity becomes normal to show.
- When you give, ask whether the gift changes a source as well as a symptom.

## 6. Build in the open

Gatekeepers decide too much of what gets built.

- Contribute to open projects, including open source software and open knowledge.
- Share what you make so others can build on it.
- Humanity's Checklist itself is open source; you can improve it with a pull request.

## Why small actions add up here

Small actions on symptoms stay small. A meal is a meal. Small actions on sources compound, because sources are systems that millions of people pass through. An introduction can become a team. A shared vision can become a plan. A changed story spreads.

That is the bet behind the checklist: not that any one person can fix the world, but that many people pushing on the same few sources can tip it.

## Start with one

Choose the item that fits you best. Writers and talkers can start with the story. Connectors can start with coordination. Builders can start with the gates. Givers can start with status. Then go to the checklist and see how your item sets off the others.
