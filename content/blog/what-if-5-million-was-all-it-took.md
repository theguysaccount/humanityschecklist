Hundreds of billions of dollars go to charity every year. Humanity's Checklist asks an uncomfortable question about all that money: what if $5 million, spent correctly, was all it actually took?

It is posed as a question on purpose. Nobody knows the answer yet. But the reasoning behind it is worth taking seriously.

## Two ways money scales

Money spent on a symptom scales roughly in a straight line. Twice the money feeds about twice as many people, treats about twice as many patients, cleans about twice as much river. That work is valuable, and it never finishes, because the system producing the need keeps running.

Money spent on a source can scale very differently. A change to a system that billions of people pass through every day reaches all of them at once, and it can keep compounding after the money is spent. That is the logic behind putting the story, coordination, vision, attention, status and gatekeepers on the checklist.

## Why the beachheads can be small

The six beachheads on the checklist were chosen partly because they can start small:

- **They are mostly digital and cultural**, so serving one more person costs almost nothing.
- **They compound.** Each new user of the Social Router or Vision Market makes it more useful to the next.
- **They are open**, so others can build on them instead of starting from scratch.
- **They need a seed, not a permanent budget.** The goal is a foothold that sustains itself, not a program that needs funding forever.

Split across six beachheads, $5 million is a focused seed for each: enough for a small team to start, not enough to waste.

## Putting $5 million in perspective

- The world spends more than $300 billion a year on designer goods, over $34 million an hour. At that pace, $5 million is under nine minutes of spending.
- Americans alone give more than $500 billion to charity each year. $5 million is about a hundred-thousandth of that.

If the bet fails, the loss is small by any standard in philanthropy. If it works, it changes how the world thinks about giving. That asymmetry is the strongest argument for trying.

## What would count as evidence

A bet like this should come with ways to tell early whether it is working. Humanity's Checklist proposes leading indicators for each item, for example:

- **Coordination:** introductions that turn into active collaborations.
- **Attention:** how people feel after using the feed, and how much of their time goes to things they can act on.
- **Status:** money given through charity designer goods.
- **Story:** the share of people who expect the future to be better, tracked over time.

Publishing these openly keeps the project honest.

## The real claim

The claim is not that $5 million will end war or reverse climate change by itself. It is that the money spent on problems is mostly aimed at the wrong level, and that a small amount aimed at the right level could move more than a large amount aimed at the wrong one.

The world does not have a charity problem. It has a coordination, vision and status problem. The resources exist. The question is where we point them.
