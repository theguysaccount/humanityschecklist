Two words have spread through discussions of the state of the world: **polycrisis** and **metacrisis**. They sound similar and are often used interchangeably, but they point at different things. The difference matters for anyone trying to do something about it.

## Polycrisis: many crises at once

A polycrisis is a situation where several crises happen together and make each other worse. A pandemic strains supply chains, which drives inflation, which fuels political anger, while a war disrupts energy and food, during a decade of climate extremes.

The term has a longer history than its recent popularity suggests. The French thinker Edgar Morin and Anne Brigitte Kern used it in the 1990s in *Homeland Earth*. The economic historian Adam Tooze helped bring it back into wide use in 2022, and the World Economic Forum's Global Risks Report featured it in 2023.

A polycrisis is a description. It says: these crises are entangled, so you cannot understand any one of them alone.

## Metacrisis: the cause underneath

A metacrisis goes one step further. It asks why so many crises are happening at once, and answers that they share common underlying drivers. The term is used by thinkers such as Daniel Schmachtenberger and Jonathan Rowson to point at the patterns that generate crises, not just the crises themselves.

If the polycrisis is the set of symptoms, the metacrisis is the disease.

## Why the distinction matters

The two terms lead to different responses.

- **If you see a polycrisis,** you try to manage many crises better: faster response, more resilience, better coordination between agencies.
- **If you see a metacrisis,** you try to change what keeps generating crises in the first place.

Both are needed. But only the second can make the list shorter.

## From metacrisis to a checklist

The metacrisis can feel too abstract to act on. Humanity's Checklist tries to make it concrete. It names six sources that sit underneath the world's crises and pairs each with one buildable project:

1. **Story:** the story of decline humanity tells itself. Beachhead: a new story of protopia.
2. **Coordination:** people who could solve problems together never meet. Beachhead: the Social Router.
3. **Vision:** we cannot picture a future worth building. Beachhead: the Vision Market.
4. **Attention:** feeds tuned for outrage. Beachhead: Bloomscroll.
5. **Status:** admiration aimed at money rather than generosity. Beachhead: charity designer goods.
6. **Gatekeepers:** a few chokepoints decide what gets built. Beachhead: the Co-Creator Portal.

These are not the only ways to describe the metacrisis, and the checklist is published openly so its sources can be challenged and improved. But a short, buildable list has one advantage over a perfect diagnosis: people can start on it this year.

## The hopeful reading

There is a hopeful side to the metacrisis idea. If crises share causes, then fixing a cause helps with many crises at once. The same structure that makes everything go wrong together can make things go right together.

That is the domino effect at the heart of Humanity's Checklist: fix the source, and watch the problems downstream begin to heal.
